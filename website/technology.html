How Remedy is built

A native iPhone app where AI reads the papers and plain, tested Swift rules make every medical decision.

  • iOS 27
  • Swift
  • SwiftUI
  • SwiftData
  • FoundationModels
  • On-device first

From paper to plan

Seven steps. AI does two of them, the patient does one, and fixed rules do the rest.

  1. CaptureDocument camera, photos, PDFs or MyChart
  2. Read textVision on the phone, read twice
  3. Hide namesFixed rules plus the on-device model
  4. DraftThe reader the patient picked writes a draft
  5. Quote checkEvery item's words must be on the page
  6. ConfirmThe patient approves each item
  7. RulesDoses, stop days, paper check, reminders
  • AI
  • Tested rules
  • The patient

Five layers, one direction

Every medical rule lives in Core, which has no SwiftUI and no SwiftData, so each rule runs and is tested on its own. Both looks draw the same view models, so Classic and Simple can't disagree.

Every saved item keeps the paper's exact words and which paper it came from. Every date is stored as days from surgery.

  • InterfaceClassic and Simple screens over one set of view models
  • ServicesAI readers, page reading, MyChart, Health, widgets, Siri, Keychain
  • PersistenceSaving, adapters, the paper check report, demo patients
  • Models16 SwiftData models under one CarePlan
  • CorePure Swift rules. No UI, no database, no AI.

AI reads. Rules decide.

If the reader the patient picked fails, Remedy says so and asks. It never switches to a cloud AI on its own.

What AI does

  • Reads a page into a draft plan
  • Finds names to hide
  • Picks which line of the papers answers a question
  • Rewords a red card's two quotes, labeled "Simpler words, not advice"

What rules decide

  • Dose times, due and missed, gaps and daily limits DoseSchedule
  • Stop and restart days RestartRule
  • When food and drink stop FastingTimes
  • Which paper to follow PaperCheck, NewerPaperRule
  • Answers and danger words AskAnswers, WarningSigns
  • Up to 60 reminders at a time ReminderPlan

Problems we had to solve

Each one is a rule in Core or a guard around the AI. Try the first two.

Papers that disagree

Which paper wins?

Every line about the same medicine, from different papers, is compared. Brand and generic names match. A blank field means the paper didn't say, never a conflict. The checks run in order and the first that applies decides.

Core/PaperCheck.swift, Core/NewerPaperRule.swift

Do the two lines say the same thing?
Do both papers have a printed date?
Does the newer line only name a group, like NSAIDs?
Printed the same day?
Is the newer paper from the surgery team?
Printed a month or more later?

Surgery dates move

Store days, not dates

Pre-op papers say "stop 3 days before surgery." Remedy stores every item that way: day 0 is surgery. When surgery is rescheduled, the patient changes one date and the whole plan moves. Drag it.

AI can make things up

Every item must quote the page

Each item the AI returns carries a quote. If those words aren't on the page, or a day number isn't written there, the item is left out and shown to the patient with the reason. The patient then confirms each item next to its quote.

Core/SourceCheck.swift, Services/AI/AIPlanConverter.swift

oxyCODONE 5 MG immediate release tablet
Take 1 tablet (5 mg total) by mouth every 4 (four) hours as needed for severe pain (7-10). Max Daily Amount: 30 mg
{Last time this was given: 5 mg on November 13, 2026 at 11:40 AM}
  • ✓
    Oxycodone 5 mg, every 4 hours as needed, 30 mg a day at mostQuote found on page 1. Goes to the patient to confirm.
  • ✕
    Ibuprofen 400 mg every 6 hoursNot on the page. Left out and listed under "We left out 1 item."

The page lines are from Ruth's sample discharge paper. The left-out item shows what happens to an invented one.

Patient details must stay on the phone

Three layers hide names first

Fixed rules catch labeled fields, record numbers, dates of birth, addresses and the names the patient typed, even when the camera misread a letter. The on-device model and NLTagger add names the rules missed. Black boxes then cover the same words on the page picture. Medicine names on a medicine line are protected.

Core/Redactor.swift, Services/AI/AIRedactor.swift, Services/Reading/PageBlackout.swift

The doctor's name stays readable, and so does the medicine. Before anything leaves the phone, the patient sees this page and can tap any word to hide or show it.

The first dose at home

Start from the hospital's last dose

Discharge papers often list when each medicine was last given. Remedy reads that line strictly: one clear time, on the paper's printed day. If AM or PM is missing it asks, and if anything is unclear it uses nothing and keeps the paper's normal times.

Core/LastGivenParser.swift, Core/DoseSchedule.swift

Take 2 tablets (1,000 mg total) by mouth every 8 (eight) hours.
{Last time this was given: 1,000 mg on November 13, 2026 at 6:02 AM}
First acetaminophen at home: 2:02 PM
Last time this was given: 5 mg at 11:40
"Was that AM or PM?" The time isn't used until the patient answers.

One medicine, many names

Match names exactly, never fuzzily

Names are cleaned and expanded through a brand table. Two lines are the same medicine when their names overlap. A group line like "NSAIDs" counts only for members the patient takes, and never beats a line that names the medicine.

Core/MedicineIdentity.swift, Core/WhatChanged.swift

Tylenol=acetaminophen
Advil=Motrin=ibuprofen
NSAIDs→ibuprofenA group line covers only the medicines on this patient's list.

Photos taken on a kitchen table

Read twice, trust neither blindly

Vision finds the page and squares it. A second copy has its shadows evened out, and both are read. The better read is kept only if it scores more than 3% higher. Any number the two reads disagree on, like 5 and 50, is shown to the patient. Camera misreads inside doses are fixed in the open.

Services/Reading/PagePreparer.swift, Core/ReadAgreement.swift

  • 1
    Find the edges and square the pageRejected if it's too small, fills the photo, or isn't shaped like paper
  • 2
    Even out shadows on a copySame size, so word boxes still line up for the blackout
  • 3
    Read both, keep the betterLines whose numbers differ become notes for the patient
  • 4
    "Remedy read '1O mg' as '10 mg'. Check it on the paper."Every fix is disclosed

Questions without guessing

Ask answers in a fixed order

Ask never sends a question to a cloud AI. The on-device model may only point at one numbered line from the papers. It can't write its own answer.

Core/AskAnswers.swift, Services/AI/Ask/AskPicker.swift

  1. Danger wordsChest pain or trouble breathing: call 911. Fever or bleeding: call the surgeon's office.
  2. Written rulesA medicine, an instruction, a visit, or what to take today, from the line the paper check follows.
  3. One line from the papersThe on-device model picks the closest line, shown word for word.
  4. Nothing found"I couldn't find that on your papers, so I won't guess."
